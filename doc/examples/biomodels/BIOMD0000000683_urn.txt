**Abstract:** 
We use mathematical models to study the relationship between HIV and the
immunesystem during the natural course of infection and in the context of differentantiviral
treatment regimes. The models suggest that an efficient cytotoxic Tlymphocyte (CTL)
memory response is required to control the virus. We define CTLmemory as long-term
persistence of CTL precursors in the absence of antigen.Infection and depletion of
CD4(+) T helper cells interfere with CTL memorygeneration, resulting in persistent
viral replication and disease progression.We find that antiviral drug therapy during
primary infection can enable thedevelopment of CTL memory. In chronically infected
patients, specific treatmentschedules, either including deliberate drug holidays
or antigenic boosts of theimmune system, can lead to a re-establishment of CTL memory.
Whether suchtreatment regimes would lead to long-term immunologic control deservesinvestigation
under carefully controlled conditions.
