Cloutier2012 - Feedback motif for Parkinson&apos;sdisease 


This model is described in the article:


[Feedback motif for the pathogenesis of Parkinson&apos;s disease.](http://identifiers.org/pubmed/22757587 "Access to this publication") 


Cloutier M, Middleton R, Wellstead P. 

IET Syst Biol 2012 Jun; 6(3): 86-93 
Abstract:


Previous article on the integrative modelling of Parkinson&apos;s disease (PD)
described a mathematical model with properties suggesting that PD pathogenesis
is associated with a feedback-induced biochemical bistability. In this article,
the authors show that the dynamics of the mathematical model can be extracted
and distilled into an equivalent two-state feedback motif whose stability properties
are controlled by multi-factorial combinations of risk factors and genetic 
mutations associated with PD. Based on this finding, the authors propose a principle
for PD pathogenesis in the form of the switch-like transition of a bistable feedback
process from &apos;healthy&apos; homeostatic levels of reactive oxygen species
and the protein ?-synuclein, to an alternative &apos;disease&apos; state in which
concentrations of both molecules are stable at the damagingly high-levels associated
with PD. The bistability is analysed using the rate curves and steady-state response
characteristics of the feedback motif. In particular, the authors show how a 
bifurcation in the feedback motif marks the pathogenic moment at which the &apos;healthy&apos;
state is lost and the &apos;disease&apos; state is initiated. Further analysis
shows how known risks (such as: age, toxins and genetic predisposition) modify
the stability characteristics of the feedback motif in a way that is compatible
with known features of PD, and which explain properties such as: multi-factorial
causality, variability in susceptibility and severity, multi-timescale progression
and the special cases of familial Parkinson&apos;s and Parkinsonian symptoms
induced purely by toxic stress.
