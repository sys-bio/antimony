ODE model describing how slight variations in Notch and Delta cellular concentrations, 
through lateral inhibition, lead to cells with different states of differentiation. 
Lateral inhibition is a process whereby a given cell adopting a given fate prevents 
its immediate neighbouring cells from doing likewise. Notch and Delta are interacting 
transmembrane proteins and according to this model, lateral inhibition is due to 
a process where the inhibited cells (where notch has been activated by Delta) loose 
their ability to inhibit other cells (by synthesizing Delta). This process creates 
a feedback loop where cells with more delta proteins on their surface inhibit their 
immediate neighbours and adopt a different cell fate than those neighbours.
