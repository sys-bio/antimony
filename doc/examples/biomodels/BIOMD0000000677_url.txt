Abstract:


A. V. Hill&apos;s 1938 paper "The heat of shortening and the dynamic constants
of muscle" is an enduring classic, presenting detailed methods, meticulous
experiments, and the model of muscle contraction that now bears Hill&apos;s name.
Pairing a simulation based on Hill&apos;s model with a reading of his paper 
allows students to follow his thought process to discover key principles of muscle
physiology and gain insight into how to develop quantitative models of physiological
processes. In this article, the experience of the author using this approach in
a graduate biomedical engineering course is outlined, along with suggestions
for adapting this approach to other audiences.
